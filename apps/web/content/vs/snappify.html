Snappify and Code2Img are both for developers who want their code to look good on a page, and they stop at very different points. Snappify builds presentations, animated walkthroughs and embeddable infographics. Code2Img makes one image, from anywhere, and gets out of the way. Compared against Snappify's own published description on 15 November 2026.

What Snappify is

Its home page asks developers to "create stunning code presentations and videos", with animation features, interactive slides whose snippets a reader can copy, multiple code windows arranged by drag and drop, rich-text and arrow annotations, and embeds for platforms such as Hashnode, Notion and Medium. It is a content-creation tool for talks, courses and social posts — closer to a design app than to an export button.

Where Code2Img is narrower, on purpose

There is no animation, no multi-window canvas, no annotation layer and no embed for a blog platform. A Code2Img card is a card: one snippet, one frame, one image or one link. If your job is a slide with two snippets side by side, an arrow pointing at the important line, or a looping typewriter animation, Snappify does that and Code2Img does not.

Where Code2Img goes further

  • Rendering as an interface. The API at /api/v1 and the MCP server let a build, a bot or an agent produce the image with no human in the editor.
  • Scale. Up to 4× on Pro, laid out at that ratio — useful when the image is going onto a printed poster or a 4K deck.
  • Share links with previews. A URL per snippet, with an Open Graph card, an expiry and deletion under your control.
  • Price and surface area. The editor needs no account; the paid plan is one price that removes the watermark and lifts the API quota.

Choosing between them

The question is not which is better, it is what the output has to do. Destination is a talk, a course or a feed where motion and annotation carry the idea: Snappify. Destination is a README, a pull request, a docs page, a changelog or an agent's answer, where the image has to be produced reliably, look right at small and large sizes, and be linkable: that is the shape Code2Img was built for.
